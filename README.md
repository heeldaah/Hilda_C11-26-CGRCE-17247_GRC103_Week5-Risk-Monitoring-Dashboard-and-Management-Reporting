## Project Overview

This project presents a risk monitoring and management reporting assessment for Northbridge Services, a fictional organisation.

The exercise analyses three months of fictional security monitoring data covering January, February and March to evaluate control performance, identify deteriorating indicators and highlight emerging operational risks.

Using Microsoft Excel, the assessment calculates key monitoring indicators, applies Green, Amber and Red thresholds, and presents performance trends through a monitoring dashboard.

It also updates the risk register, proposes corrective actions with accountable owners and verification measures, and communicates the findings through a concise management report.

## Objectives

- Calculate key monitoring and control-performance indicators.
- Apply defined Green, Amber and Red classification thresholds.
- Analyse three months of monitoring data and identify deteriorating control performance.
- Develop a dashboard showing monthly trends and threshold classifications.
- Identify and prioritise significant operational and security risks.
- Update the risk register using likelihood and impact assessments.
- Recommend corrective actions with accountable owners, deadlines and closure evidence.
- Prepare a management report highlighting key findings and decisions required.

## Monitoring Indicators

The assessment evaluates five key indicators:

1. **Monitoring Coverage:** Percentage of eligible endpoints actively monitored.
2. **Timely Critical-Alert Review:** Percentage of critical alerts reviewed within 24 hours.
3. **Restore-Test Success:** Percentage of completed restoration tests that succeeded.
4. **Overdue High-Risk Actions:** Percentage of high-risk treatment actions overdue at month-end.
5. **Confirmed Unauthorised Changes:** Number of confirmed unauthorised file changes.

Each indicator is assessed against the thresholds supplied in the laboratory brief.

## Dashboard and Risk Analysis

The Excel dashboard presents the three-month performance trends and highlights areas requiring management attention.

The March analysis identifies three priority concerns:

- **Monitoring blind spots:** Monitoring coverage declined to 90%, leaving 12 eligible endpoints without active monitoring.
- **Delayed alert review:** Only 70% of critical alerts were reviewed within 24 hours.
- **Weakening recovery and risk treatment:** Restore-test success declined to 80%, while the overdue high-risk action rate increased to 40%.

These findings inform the risk register and proposed management interventions.

## Risk Treatment and Action Planning

Four priority risks are documented in the risk register:

- Endpoint monitoring blind spots.
- Delayed critical-alert review.
- Failed backup restoration.
- Overdue high-risk treatment actions.

Each risk includes supporting March evidence, an existing control assessment, proposed likelihood and impact ratings, a risk score, an accountable owner, and a recommended action.

The action plan also defines target dates, closure evidence and outcome-based verification measures to support effective remediation and assurance.

## Key Recommendations

- Prioritise onboarding the 12 endpoints without active monitoring.
- Improve critical-alert review processes to meet the 24-hour target.
- Investigate failed restoration tests and validate recovery through successful retesting.
- Review overdue high-risk actions and establish clear accountability.
- Introduce regular monitoring, escalation and management reporting.
- Verify remediation through measurable outcomes and supporting evidence.

## Methodology and Limitations

The assessment uses the fictional dataset and classification thresholds provided in the official laboratory brief.

Indicator calculations are formula-driven, with percentages displayed to one decimal place. Risk scores are determined using likelihood multiplied by impact, rather than being assigned automatically based on indicator classifications.

The proposed risk ratings, existing control descriptions, owners and remediation deadlines are exercise-based assessments and recommendations.

The analysis is limited to three monthly observations. Consequently, the results identify observed performance changes but cannot establish a reliable long-term forecast or confirm the underlying causes of the deterioration.

## Conclusion

The assessment demonstrates the importance of continuous risk monitoring, effective control-performance measurement and timely management reporting.

The findings highlight weaknesses in endpoint monitoring, alert response, restoration capability and risk treatment governance.

By translating monitoring results into prioritised risks, accountable actions and measurable verification requirements, Northbridge Services can strengthen its control environment and improve operational resilience.
